# CMO Align

Presented by Project Pōpolo.

Working brief. Hosted on the Pōpolo site. CMOs log in, onboard, and work sessions asynchronously.

---

## Problem

Referee coaches (CMOs) do not apply the same standard.

Given the same community-referee performance, one CMO will call it exceptional, another will call it below standard, and a third will meet in the middle — and they will coach three different things. That inconsistency shows up in grades, in what gets prioritized, and in what a referee actually hears on the weekend.

A live calibration call does not fix this at scale. Twenty to forty CMOs, different time zones, college schedules: that is not a working meeting. It is a meeting that nobody can all attend, and it is too large to produce a standard anyway.

The work that *does* produce a standard is small: a handful of coaches watching the same clip, arguing, agreeing the coaching list, and agreeing the grade. Everyone else needs a way to measure against that standard on their own time — without seeing the answer first.

---

## Goal

A shared evaluation standard among CMOs: not identical reports, and not a 40-person Zoom.

The program exists so that when a CMO watches a community referee, they notice what matters at that referee’s level, choose two or three interventions that would actually move them, and assign a grade that would hold up against a common bar.

The goal is not “getting everyone to agree in a room.” The goal is that independent coaches, working asynchronously, land in the same band — on grade *and* on what they would coach.

---

## Objectives

1. **Set the standard in a small room.** A core panel of four to five CMOs reviews footage together, agrees the coaching priorities, and agrees the grade. That panel output is the answer key.

2. **Let the rest of the group calibrate without a meeting.** The wider cohort (college coaches and others) logs in, watches the same clip independently, submits a structured coaching report and grade, then sees how they compare to the panel.

3. **Protect independence.** A CMO cannot see the panel’s grade, coaching list, or anyone else’s submission until they have submitted their own. Comparison comes after, not before.

4. **Calibrate coaching, not just scores.** Matching a number is shallow. The useful test is whether they identified the same development priorities. Reveal shows the panel’s list next to theirs.

5. **Make it usable across time zones.** Multiple objects (clips) live in the program at once. CMOs work them on their own schedule. One monthly live call for forty people is not the product; a bank of objects against a known standard is.

6. **Use real community referees.** Footage is of developing officials, not elite theory. The CMO is answering: *If this were the referee I was assigned this weekend, what would I actually tell them?*

7. **Use the real report, not notes.** Submissions follow the same structure expected in the field: observation → interpretation → coaching priority → intervention → grade.

---

## How it runs (at a glance)

```
Core panel (4–5)     live / tight loop
  same footage → discuss → agree coaching list + grade
                         ↓
                   published standard
                         ↓
Cohort (20–40)       async, logged-in
  watch → independent report → reveal → compare to panel
```

The site hosts the cohort loop: login, CMO session, objects to grade, lock until submit, then reveal. The core panel still meets; that conversation is how the standard gets made. The cohort never needs that call.

A later layer (not required to start): with referee consent, a calibrated coaching report can attach to that official’s profile so the exercise also leaves a development record.

## Name

**CMO Align**, presented by Project Pōpolo.

Locked. No acronym. CCP is retired.

On the site: nav and session home say **CMO Align**. Credit line: *Presented by Project Pōpolo.*

Inside the program, keep these labels:

- **Core panel** — the four to five who set the standard on a given object.
- **Cohort** — everyone else, working async.
- **Object** — one clip + context + report + panel answer key.
- **Reveal** — the moment after submit when the standard is shown.
- **The standard** — the panel’s agreed grade and coaching list. “On standard” / “off standard” is how we talk about a CMO’s result, not a second program name.
